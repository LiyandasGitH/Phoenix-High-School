Our history

Phoenix was not built in comfortable times, and that history still shapes who we are.

  1. 1966

    Manenberg is established

    The apartheid government creates Manenberg as a township for low-income Coloured families removed from District Six and Constantia. The community's story begins with forced displacement.

  2. 1983

    Phoenix opens its doors

    Phoenix Secondary School is founded on Silverstream Road as a dual-medium school offering Grades 8 to 12 in both English and Afrikaans — a decision that still defines how the school serves its community.

  3. 1980s

    Forged in the struggle

    Manenberg's anti-apartheid activism is nurtured at its three high schools — Phoenix High, Silverstream High and Manenberg High — by politically aware educators. Learners and teachers organise through the Manenberg Action Student Congress (MASCO) and the Manenberg Action Committee (MAC), using music, drama and poetry to speak against the regime.

  4. 2009

    A partnership with Germany begins

    The Kreisgymnasium Heinsberg in North Rhine-Westphalia opens a learner exchange with Phoenix. Seventeen years later it is still running.

  5. 2010

    The Phoenix Cycling Club

    Bicycles donated through the Aachen–Cape Town partnership become a school cycling club, giving learners a way to travel safely to and from school.

  6. 2011

    The school writes its name on the wall

    A joint mural project with German partners paints the Phoenix name and crest across a front wall of the building, visible from the surrounding streets.

  7. 2018

    Balls Not Guns launches at Phoenix

    The street-sports initiative is officially launched at our school, and a Phoenix educator serves as its coordinator. Its leagues still play their finals on our fields.

  8. 2025

    76.9% — and rising

    Our National Senior Certificate pass rate climbs to 76.9%, up from 64.2% in 2023, while the school hosts its fourth annual Manenberg career expo with Manenberg SAPS, the WCED and Safe Schools.

  9. 2026

    Welcomed in Heinsberg

    A Phoenix delegation is officially received at the Kreis Heinsberg district office in Germany, after seventeen years of exchange.

Our name

Why "Phoenix"?

A phoenix is a bird that burns and rises again from its own ashes. For a school built for a community that was displaced and then left to rebuild itself, the name was never decorative. It is a promise.

"As long as the community keeps the flame of hope alive at Phoenix High, we will rise from the ashes and soar to the skies once again."

— Shameeg Fredericks, Principal
43 years

Still here, still teaching

Since 1983 this school has graduated learners through a state of emergency, a transition to democracy, and every wave of change in Manenberg since. Generations of families in this community are Phoenix families — parents and grandparents who sat in the same classrooms their children sit in today.

Welcome to Phoenix Secondary School.

When people hear the name Manenberg, they often think first of gangsterism, vandalism and substance abuse. We are working every day to change that perception — and to make this school the beacon of hope and resilience it has always been for this community.

Our learners grow up inside real and difficult circumstances. Our task is not to pretend otherwise. Our task is to give every one of them a quality education, a safe place to be during the day, and adults who believe in them without conditions. We do that in partnership: with parents, with the police, with the Western Cape Education Department, with NGOs and neighbourhood watches, and with our friends at the Kreisgymnasium Heinsberg in Germany.

To parents considering Phoenix for your child: come and see the school. Meet the teachers. Look at what our learners achieve in a classroom, on a soccer field, and on a stage. And to our learners: your future is not in a gang, and it is not decided for you. It is built here, one subject, one practice, one day at a time.

As long as the community keeps the flame of hope alive at Phoenix, we will rise from the ashes and soar to the skies once again.

Shameeg Fredericks Principal, Phoenix Secondary School

Meet Our Team

Who we are

A dual-medium, no-nonsense, community-first secondary school.

Identity

Dual-medium, one school

Phoenix teaches Grades 8 to 12 in both English and Afrikaans. For families on the Cape Flats, that matters: a learner can be taught in the language they think in, and parents can follow their child's schooling in their own language. Being dual-medium is one of the clearest expressions of who we serve.

Ethos

Building Brighter Futures

Our motto is a statement of intent rather than a slogan. It commits us to three things: that learning comes first, that every learner is treated with dignity, and that the school belongs to the community around it.

Learning

Academic work is the core of the school day, not an add-on.

Respect

Learners, staff and parents treat one another with dignity.

Responsibility

We own our choices, our work and our part in the community.

Participation

Sport, culture and service belong to a complete education.

Our community

Manenberg is not the background to this school. It is the reason for it.

Phoenix sits in the middle of Manenberg, a community built by forced removals in 1966 and shaped ever since by poverty, unemployment and gang violence — and equally by churches, neighbourhood watches, sports clubs, and generations of families who refused to give up on the place.

We do not describe our learners by their circumstances. We describe them by what they do: the matriculants who pass, the soccer teams that beat schools with far more resources, the learners who board a plane to Germany, and the old scholars who come back and teach here.

What we ask of the community is simple — keep the flame of hope alive. What we owe the community in return is a school that is worth that hope.
